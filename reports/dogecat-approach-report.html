DOGECAT 🐕🐈

Bringing BIP-347 (OP_CAT), Taproot, and BIP-360 (P2MR) to Dogecoin: an evidence-based comparison of engineering approaches, with a recommendation.
Prepared for Udi · June 12, 2026 · rev 2 (adds §5-E stretch goal, compressed timeline) · Empirical data measured directly from the dogecoin/dogecoin and bitcoin/bitcoin git histories · All claims sourced (§9)
⚡ The Verdict

You are not insane. This is hard but newly feasible — and the bottleneck is not the one you feared. The "Dogecoin essence" turns out to be tiny: when Dogecoin's own maintainers re-applied everything that makes Dogecoin special onto Bitcoin Core 0.21 (their abandoned 1.21-dev branch), the entire delta was ~5,600 lines in src/ — of which only ~1,600 are consensus-critical (AuxPoW, Scrypt, Digishield, subsidy, chainparams). Re-implementing that on top of dogecat's current Core master is squarely within reach of AI-assisted engineering, and Namecoin proves the maintenance model works in production.

The real costs are: (1) proving bit-for-bit consensus compatibility from genesis — a testing problem, not a porting problem; (2) the activation bundle is bigger than you remembered, because Dogecoin never activated SegWit or CSV — both are hard-disabled in their chainparams; and (3) the social layer — Dogecoin has ~2 consensus-grade reviewers and an 18-month release gap. Our recommendation (§6): build dogecat as a parallel "Dogecoin Inquisition"-style client, prove it with a differential consensus-testing harness, demo the bundle on a public doge-signet, and only then bring an activation proposal to the Dogecoin community — framed around quantum resistance, where they already have live interest.

§2Ground Truth: Where Dogecoin Actually Is

2.1 Consensus state — further back than you remembered

Your recollection was that Dogecoin had SegWit. It does not. From mainnet chainparams.cpp (v1.14.9 and master):

  • SegWit deployment: nTimeout = 0; // Disabled. The 1.21-dev branch is even blunter: SegwitHeight = max_int; // Disabled on Dogecoin.
  • CSV (BIP 68/112/113): deployment params are copy-pasted Bitcoin dates that expired in May 2017, never signaled on Dogecoin. 1.21-dev: CSVHeight = max_int; // Disabled on Dogecoin and // Dogecoin does not use BIP9.
  • Last activated consensus change: BIP65 (CLTV) via the 1.14.0 soft fork at height 3,464,751 — in 2019.
  • What IS special and live: Scrypt PoW, AuxPoW merged mining since block 371,337 (chain ID 0x62), Digishield difficulty, 1-minute blocks, fixed 10,000 DOGE/block tail emission, era-dependent consensus params implemented as a linked list of Consensus::Params.

Implication: the activation bundle is not "Taproot + CAT + 360". It is realistically CSV + SegWit + Taproot + BIP-347 (+ BIP-360/P2MR) — four-to-five soft-fork generations in one program. The silver lining: every one of them except 347/360 is battle-tested Bitcoin code that already ships, fully integrated and tested, inside dogecat's codebase today. The activation bundle is big socially, but the marginal code is nearly zero in Approach A.

2.2 The maintainer situation

  • Consensus-grade reviewers: effectively Patrick Lodder + chromatic (plus xanimo and volunteers). Latest release 1.14.9 shipped Dec 2024 — an 18-month gap and counting. Master carries an explicit "unstable" warning.
  • They already tried your Approach A once. The 1.21 plan (2021) was exactly "re-apply the Dogecoin diff onto Bitcoin Core 0.21." It stalled; in Feb 2024 Lodder declared it lacked momentum ("one serial stream of risky must-have, blocking patches") and pivoted to a modest 1.15 (CSV activation, dependency cleanup) — which has also shipped nothing in 2+ years. Diagnosis: review bandwidth, not code production. That is precisely the constraint AI-era tooling attacks.
  • The Dec 2024 "DogeReaper" incident (a crash bug inherited from old upstream code took down ~69% of nodes; ~87% hadn't patched after 10 days) cuts both ways: it's the strongest argument for getting Dogecoin onto a maintained modern base, and a warning that activation windows must be long.

2.3 The openings (this is the good news)

✓ Three live on-ramps

1. Taproot thread: In dogecoin/dogecoin discussion #3684 (Nov 2024), Lodder: "I know of multiple people that are working in the background to propose enhancements that could ultimately enable taproot." He floated accepting witness segregation "but DO NOT provide any economic incentives or extra block size" — i.e., SegWit without the witness discount. That's a concrete, named design preference we can build to.
2. Quantum: The Dogecoin Foundation (incl. Michi Lumin) already executed an experimental post-quantum-secured transaction on Dogecoin mainnet ("RE-EN", via OP_RETURN commitments). The quantum narrative has internal champions before we say a word.
3. CSV is already their plan: Lodder proposed activating CSV with 1.15 (discussion #3509). Our bundle's first stage is their own roadmap — we'd be accelerating it, not hijacking it.

ℹ The quantum-cat story, honestly

The "OP_CAT enables quantum-proof Dogecoin" pitch is real but must be framed carefully. Verified: Lamport/Winternitz one-time hash-based signatures genuinely work in tapscript with CAT (Rubin 2021, Heilman 2024; a working Winternitz prototype runs on bitcoin-inquisition at ~2,070 vbytes/input — ~20× a Schnorr input). STARK verification via CAT works but burns ~78% of a block per proof (StarkWare, signet). The catch, stated in BIP-347 itself: CAT-based PQ signatures inside a P2TR leaf are worthless alone, because a quantum attacker spends via the taproot key path (NUMS points don't save you). BIP-360/P2MR is the prerequisite that makes the CAT quantum story true. That's why the bundle is coherent: Taproot gives script trees, P2MR removes the quantum hole, CAT supplies the signature primitive. Doge + CAT + quantum armor is a defensible technical narrative, not just a meme — but oversell it and the first cryptographer in the room will torch us with the key-path caveat, so we say it precisely.

§5The Approaches

A

Port the Dogecoin Essence onto Latest Core ("the dogecat plan")

RECOMMENDED
Your proposal. The Namecoin model. Enumerate everything that makes Dogecoin ≠ Bitcoin; re-implement it on dogecat (Core 31.99); prove chain equivalence with a differential test harness.
dogecat = Core master＋essence: AuxPoW ⋅ Scrypt ⋅ Digishield ⋅ subsidy ⋅ params➜full IBD parity vs 1.14.9➜flip on bundle

The essence is already enumerated for us — twice. The 1.14.9-vs-Core-0.14 diff gives the raw superset; the maintainers' own 1.21-dev branch gives a curated, maintainer-blessed ~5.6k-line port targeting a much closer Core (0.21 already has taproot, versionbits v2, the modern wallet). We port from 1.21-dev's structure, not from scratch. Taproot, SegWit, CSV, the fuzz/functional-test infrastructure, assumeutxo, libsecp256k1 with schnorr — all already in the base. The marginal payload is BIP-347 (+34/−6 lines from inquisition) and P2MR when it stabilizes.

Pros

  • Smallest novel-code surface. Everything consensus-dangerous (taproot, segwit) is verbatim, maximally-reviewed Bitcoin code. The new code is AuxPoW/Scrypt/Digishield integration — well-understood, and reviewable in one sitting (~1.6k lines).
  • Proven model: Namecoin re-applies its patch set onto every Core major (currently nc31.0). Perpetual: future Core security fixes come nearly free.
  • Inherits modern testing: fuzz targets, functional test framework, CI — the harness for proving equivalence largely exists.
  • Fixes the DogeReaper class of problem (ancient inherited bugs) as a side effect — a genuine gift to Dogecoin, independent of CAT.
  • AI-leverage is maximal: enumeration, porting, and test generation are exactly what agents are good at; review artifacts (annotated diffs, equivalence proofs) can be produced for human reviewers.

Cons

  • Full-validation-from-genesis is unforgiving: every historical quirk (pre-AuxPoW eras, the params linked-list, v1/v2 block oddities, BIP34-never-in-v2 weirdness, legacy serialization) must be byte-perfect. Mitigated by differential sync testing — this is where most of the engineering actually goes.
  • Politically reads as "replace Dogecoin Core wholesale." A whole-codebase swap is a bigger ask than a feature PR — must be packaged as a parallel client first (see §6), not a hostile reimplementation.
  • Core master moved a lot since 0.21 (kernel split, CMake, descriptor wallets): the 1.21-dev patch placement is a map, not a mechanical patch-apply.
  • Non-consensus surface (RPC compat, wallet formats, Qt branding, AuxPoW mining RPCs createauxblock/submitauxblock) is real work beyond the 1.6k consensus lines.
B

Backport the Features into Dogecoin 1.14.x

NOT RECOMMENDED
Bring CSV → SegWit → Taproot → CAT into the existing Core-0.14-era Dogecoin tree, feature by feature.
Dogecoin 1.14.x (2017 base)⬅hand-backport CSV⬅hand-backport SegWit⬅hand-backport Taproot across 7 majors of refactors

Pros

  • Minimal disturbance to the code maintainers already trust; review is per-feature.
  • Matches their own incremental instinct (CSV-in-1.15 plan).
  • No chain-equivalence proof needed — it's the same node, plus features.

Cons

  • Creates exactly what killed Litecoin's MWEB: large, novel, lightly-reviewed consensus code. Taproot touches script interpretation, sighashing, libsecp256k1, p2p, wallet, mempool — backporting it across 7 majors of refactoring means hand-rewriting it into an alien codebase. The result is no longer "Bitcoin's reviewed taproot"; it's our bespoke taproot. (Litecoin's bespoke MWEB: ~85k LTC inflation exploit + 13-block reorg, March 2026.)
  • The 0.14 base lacks the modern test/fuzz infrastructure to validate it with.
  • Each backport deepens divergence from upstream forever; the rebase debt compounds.
  • Dogecoin's own maintainers concluded even modest upgrades to this tree are a "serial stream of risky must-have, blocking patches."
C

Resurrect 1.21-dev, Then Add the Bundle

VIABLE FALLBACK / POLITICAL BRIDGE
Finish the maintainers' stalled rebase (Dogecoin on Core 0.21), activate the bundle there. Taproot code is already in that tree, marked NEVER_ACTIVE.
1.21-dev (stalled Feb 2024)➜finish + harden➜activate bundle on Core-0.21-era base➜…rebase again later anyway

Pros

  • Maximum political continuity: "we finished your project" beats "we replaced your project."
  • Taproot + versionbits already integrated by the maintainers themselves; smallest distance to a taproot-capable Dogecoin.
  • Useful regardless: 1.21-dev is our porting map for Approach A.

Cons

  • Core 0.21 is EOL upstream — no security maintenance; 5+ years of fixes (incl. everything learned post-taproot) must be cherry-picked by hand, forever.
  • Inherits whatever made it stall, minus none of the review burden.
  • Buys a codebase that needs the same painful rebase again in 2–3 years — pays the equivalence-testing cost without landing on a maintained base.
  • P2MR reference work targets current Core; backporting it to 0.21 is bespoke consensus code again.
D

True Rebase: Replay Bitcoin History Through Dogecoin's Tree

NOT RECOMMENDED
Literally rebase 14,539 upstream commits through Dogecoin's history (or merge upstream repeatedly), preserving git lineage.

Pros

  • Preserves continuous git history/provenance; conceptually "the same repo, updated."
  • Conflict-by-conflict review trail.

Cons

  • ~615k lines of drift; thousands of conflicting commits; build system changed twice (autotools→CMake). The conflict-resolution stream is itself unreviewable.
  • Ends at the same destination as Approach A with 10× the labor and a messier audit trail. Strictly dominated.
E

Stretch Goal: a From-Scratch "Next-Gen" Dogecoin Node

consensus from scratch: NO everything-else from scratch, around the dogecat kernel: YES
Added after review (June 12, rev 2). A second, modern node implementation ("all the bells and whistles devs would have wanted"), proven by the same differential harness — possibly proposed alongside the Core-31-based dogecat.

The evidence against from-scratch consensus is brutal. btcd — the most mature Bitcoin reimplementation, a decade of maintenance, Core-PR tracking, test vectors, fuzzing — shipped CVE-2024-38365 (a FindAndDelete() mismatch any user could exploit to split the chain) which hid for ~9 years, and stalled live on mainnet twice in October 2022. rust-bitcoin's own README forbids consensus use of itself. Differential testing finds divergences but can never prove their absence — and there is no formal spec to verify against; the code is the spec. Worse for us specifically: no production alternative full node has ever existed for any AuxPoW chain — Namecoin's and Viacoin's btcd forks both died of upstream drift — and nobody has ever shipped an alternative Dogecoin full node at all (libdogecoin is a wallet library + SPV tool; even the Foundation's Dogebox runs stock Dogecoin Core). Scrypt+AuxPoW header validation, the most divergence-prone part, is exactly the part with no battle-tested code to borrow. And strategically: our entire activation pitch is "zero novel consensus code" — shipping a from-scratch consensus engine next to it hands critics the Litecoin-MWEB stick we built Approach A to avoid.

But the dream survives — through the kernel. Bitcoin Core merged the libbitcoinkernel C API into master in November 2025 (it is already sitting in dogecat's tree), exposing Core's exact consensus/validation engine as a linkable library. Floresta — the modern Rust node — ripped out its own script validation in April 2026 and now wraps the kernel instead of reimplementing it; TheCharlatan's kernel-node is a Rust node with Core's engine inside, syncing signet today. Approach A makes the Dogecoin version of this fall out nearly free: keep the essence code kernel-side, and dogecat yields "dogekernel" — Dogecoin consensus as an embeddable library, a first for any AuxPoW chain. The next-gen node then reimplements everything that is allowed to be creative (Rust, modern async P2P, built-in explorer/Electrum/indexes, slick APIs, payment-oriented UX) around a consensus core that is dogecat's by construction, not by testing.

dogecat (Core 31.99 + essence)⇣ extractsdogekernel (consensus lib)⇠ embedsnext-gen node: from-scratch P2P ⋅ mempool ⋅ wallet ⋅ explorer ⋅ APIs

Why it's worth doing (as phase 2)

  • "Two node implementations, one consensus engine, zero divergence by construction" — a better multi-client story than Ethereum's, sized for a chain with two reviewers.
  • Massive dev-appeal / meme surface; gives the campaign a second act after signet launch.
  • Parallelizable with token spend; never blocks or endangers the activation path.
  • dogekernel itself is a gift to the ecosystem (exchanges, indexers, wallets embed real validation — Floresta-style consumers become possible on Dogecoin).
  • No soft-fork lag: independent reimplementations chronically trail consensus changes (btcd merged taproot validation ~4 months after Bitcoin's activation; its segwit port took 16 months of review). A kernel-sharing client enforces new rules the moment dogecat does — critical for a chain about to do five soft forks.

Caveats

  • Kernel API status (verified in-tree): the C API source is in the tagged v31.0 release (Apr 15, 2026), but behind an experimental, default-OFF build flag (-DBUILD_KERNEL_LIB=ON); the header is unversioned ("expect breaking changes") and the compiled library isn't shipped in Core's release binaries. Non-blocking for us — dogecat ships its own binaries — but expect API churn until ~v32+.
  • Kernel covers consensus/chainstate only — mempool policy is reimplemented, so relay-level (not chain-level) divergence needs its own differential tests.
  • The activation proposal must rest on dogecat alone; the next-gen node is ecosystem candy, never the consensus vehicle. Announce staged, build parallel.
  • Expect Satoshi's 2010 line — "a second, compatible implementation of Bitcoin … would be a menace to the network" — to be quoted at us. The prepared answer: a kernel-sharing client is not a second consensus implementation; it is the same implementation wearing different clothes. (Greg Maxwell's 2018 post-CVE argument against multiple implementations attacks independent reimplementation of consensus rules — which is exactly the part we don't do.)

§6Recommendation: A, Packaged as "Dogecoin Inquisition"

Approach A wins on every engineering axis; its only real weakness is political ("you replaced our codebase"). So we don't ask anyone to merge it on day one. We copy AJ Towns' bitcoin-inquisition playbook: dogecat ships as an independent, openly-developed parallel client + public signet where the bundle is live and developers can play with CAT-on-doge. Demos, wallets, and quantum-signature PoCs create the demand; the differential-testing evidence creates the trust; adoption (or upstreaming, or maintainer endorsement) becomes the community's choice rather than our demand. Meanwhile we offer Dogecoin Core genuinely useful artifacts along the way (the equivalence test harness works on their node too; CSV activation tooling serves their existing 1.15 plan).

Essence enumeration & specdays · mostly done already
Completeness by exhaustion, not by trusting 1.21-dev. The authoritative source is the full v1.14.9 ↔ Core-0.14-ancestor diff (14,418 Dogecoin-side commits; ~71k changed lines in src/). Two-pass provenance accounting: (a) commit-level — git patch-id matching against the entire bitcoin/bitcoin history (both histories now live in one repo) automatically identifies the thousands of cherry-picked upstream backports; (b) hunk-level — every line of the residual diff gets classified as essence / branding / build / test / dead, until nothing is unaccounted for. The essence list is the residue of that accounting. The 1.21-dev curated diff is then used as an independent cross-check (discrepancies in either direction are investigated, not assumed away), alongside 1.14.x release notes. The behavioral oracles of Phase 2 are the final backstop: a missed consensus item surfaces loudly as IBD divergence; missed policy/RPC items are quiet, which is why mempool-acceptance and RPC-surface differential tests exist separately. Differential consensus harness + the visual proof layer — the centerpiecev1 in ~2–4 wks · soak runs forever after
This is what makes the whole thing credible, and it is compute-bound, not authoring-bound: each full differential IBD vs Dogecoin Core 1.14.9 (~6M blocks) takes on the order of a day, and the loop is sync → diverge at some 2014-era block → fix → resume-from-snapshot. (a) Lockstep IBD comparing every block/UTXO-set hash; (b) differential fuzzing of script/tx/block acceptance — parser/script-level via the bitcoinfuzz pattern (71 cross-implementation bugs found on Bitcoin), stateful whole-node level by extending Brink's Fuzzamoto (snapshot fuzzing, explicitly implementation-agnostic); (c) historical edge-case replay; (d) shadow-node soak on live mainnet. Note: continuous whole-chain UTXO-state equivalence testing between implementations does not exist anywhere today — even on Bitcoin it's parser-level fuzzing plus reused test vectors. Building it is extra work but makes the harness itself a first, citable contribution. Front it with public instruments that double as the campaign: a live parity dashboard (both nodes syncing side-by-side, every block hash compared — "5,9xx,xxx / 5,9xx,xxx identical · 100.000%", screenshot-able by normies) and an annotated diff explorer (every line dogecat changes vs Bitcoin Core, tagged to its essence item with a plain-English explanation and a side-by-side link to the 1.14.9 code it reimplements — the entire novel surface walkable in an afternoon). The meme layer sits on real instruments.

Honest overall timeline — three different clocks: Authoring time (where AI changes everything): the port, the signet, the dashboards — days to ~2 weeks each, heavily parallelizable. Credibility time (where AI changes nothing): "synced identically once" is a demo; "N months of lockstep mainnet operation + X CPU-years of differential fuzzing, watch it live" is an argument. That clock starts at week 4, matures in the background while the campaign runs, and gates activation (still community-paced, 12–24+ months) — not launch.
